# Snap · Social marketing playbook — Facebook, LinkedIn, X (and what outperforms them)

Companion to `GTM-PLAYBOOK.md` (website outreach) and `SNAP-COMPETITIVE-ANALYSIS.md` (the math, migration pools,
and competitor weaknesses referenced throughout). Written for a solo founder, near-zero budget, product-led.

---

## 0. The strategy in one paragraph

Photographers don't buy studio software from ads — they buy it from **other photographers' complaints answered
well**. Every competitor in the analysis has a public wound: Dubsado's +75% price hike, HoneyBook's 3.4% autopay
fee, Zenfolio's support collapse, Sprout's "galleries suck" threads, VSCO's forced $499 bundle, Studio Ninja's
walled garden, Pixieset's 15% free-tier commission. Your marketing is showing up where those wounds are discussed
with **proof** (a live demo gallery, honest math, shipped features) — not with brand posts. Everything below
executes one sentence: **be the founder who answers the pricing/software question better than anyone, with a
product link that finally earns the click.**

Three force multipliers before spending a minute on any channel:

1. **A public demo gallery** — a real sample shoot behind your OTP flow (`/g/demo-token`), linked everywhere.
   It demos the wow *and* the security model in one click. This is your "link in bio" everywhere.
2. **Switch/comparison pages** — one page per migration pool (Zenfolio, Táve/VSCO, Dubsado, HoneyBook, Sprout,
   Studio Ninja, Pixieset) with the honest 2026 math from the competitive analysis. Every social post links
   here, not to the homepage. They also rank in search within months.
3. **UTM discipline in the dashboard you already have** — every link carries `?utm_src=fbgroup|linkedin|x|...`;
   log social-sourced signups as leads. You already built a pipeline tracker; use it for marketing, not just outreach.

## 1. Channel reality check (so you spend time where photographers actually are)

Photographer-buyer density, best first: **Instagram ≈ Facebook groups > Reddit > YouTube/SEO > X > LinkedIn.**
You asked for FB/LI/X — full playbook below — but:

- **Instagram** is non-negotiable later (the product is pictures; demo clips repost as Reels for free).
- **Reddit** is where every migration pool literally threads (r/WeddingPhotography, r/photography). Play it
  carefully: disclose "I'm the founder" always, answer helpfully, no link spam — one good answer in a
  "Sprout galleries suck" thread outconverts a week of posting.
- **YouTube/SEO** compounds: "Pixieset vs ShootProof 2026" comparison videos/articles rank and convert for years.
  You already own the research to make the honest best-in-class ones.

Treat FB/LI/X as the conversation layer; IG/Reddit/SEO as the capture layer.

## 2. Content pillars (the only five things you post about)

| Pillar | What it is | Example hooks (from the research) |
|---|---|---|
| **Money math** | Commission + stack-cost breakdowns with real numbers | "Pixieset's free tier keeps 15% — on a $2,400 print sale that's $360. I charge $0 commission and $15/mo." · "Dubsado Starter went $20→$35 in Dec. Here's what $35 doesn't include." · "HoneyBook's autopay rate is 3.4%, not the 2.9% on the homepage." |
| **Security stories** | Leaked-link horror → OTP vault demo | "A bride's gallery link leaked to her wedding FB group. Here's what 'revoke in one click' looks like." |
| **Build in public** | Ship notes with screenshots/30s videos, honest metrics | "Shipped today: digital download sales, 0% commission, your Stripe. Here's the 40-second flow." |

Rule: every pillar ends at the demo gallery or a switch page — never a naked homepage link.

## 3. Facebook — your deepest of the three

### 3a. Groups (the actual goldmine)
- Join 10–15 groups where your ICP talks shop: wedding-photographer *business* groups (not gear groups),
  portrait/newborn business groups, "photographers running a business" communities, Rising Tide Society /
  TuesdaysTogether chapters, regional groups (Sask/Canada photographers first — easy local proof).
- **The 80/20 rule:** 80% of activity = answering software/pricing/workflow questions with genuinely useful
  detail, zero links. 20% = a post from you (demo video, math graphic, ship note). Many groups ban
  self-promo — your profile (pinned "what I'm building" post + demo gallery link) does the funneling.
- **Trigger threads are conversions:** every group runs "what do you use for galleries/CRM?" weekly. Answer with
  the honest comparison (including when a competitor is the right answer — that credibility is the moat), and
  if the OP asks, DM the demo link. One helpful answer in a pricing thread keeps converting for months (they rank
  in group search).
- Monthly value post: a genuinely useful artifact (stack-cost worksheet, contract-clause checklist, gallery
  security checklist) — no pitch, CTA in comments only.
- At ~500 email subscribers, **start your own group**: "Pricing & Software for Photographers" — you now own the
  venue where the buying conversation happens.

### 3b. Facebook page + paid (only after organic signal)
- Page = reposts of demo clips + ship notes; it exists for ads credibility, not reach.
- **Ads:** start at $5–10/day after week 4, only on proven organic creative. Objective: traffic to the stack-cost
  calculator (email capture) or a switch page. Audiences: interest stacks (wedding photography, small business),
  your email list as custom audience, then 1% lookalike. Creative: 15s vertical demo clips and price-math
  graphics. Never mention competitors by name in ad copy — do it in organic comparisons where nuance is allowed.
- Budget reality: $150–300/mo is enough to validate; kill anything above $2/signup-cost for an email.

## 4. LinkedIn — lowest photographer density, best build-in-public reach

### 4a. Who you're actually talking to
Full-time studio owners (business people first), studio managers, volume/school & sports photography companies,
wedding educators, second shooters going pro, and — importantly — other indie SaaS founders who amplify.
Position: **"solo dev in Saskatoon building the anti-commission photography platform."** The underdog-vs-giant
frame (snap at $29 vs HoneyBook raising 89%) is LinkedIn-native storytelling.

### 4b. Mechanics
- Post from your **personal** profile 3×/week (Mon build note, Wed money-math, Fri demo clip). The Snap company
  page only mirrors — 0-follower company pages get zero reach.
- **Comments > posts at the start:** 15 min/day leaving substantive comments on posts by photo-business
  educators and in founder threads. At <1k followers, reach is earned in other people's comment sections.
- Post format that works: 1 hook line → 3–8 short lines → one screenshot → one question. Never external links in
  the post body (reach killer) — link in comments or "DM me for the demo."
- **DM funnel:** commenter → DM with the demo gallery link ("made this for exactly this problem — 30 seconds to
  click through") → offer to import their first gallery free. This mirrors your proof-first website playbook.
- LinkedIn ads: **skip** ($8–15+ CPL for SMB SaaS; wrong density). Organic only.

## 5. X / Twitter — smallest crowd, best launchpad

- Primary account = you (founder, build-in-public); secondary = @snap handle as a changelog mirror.
- Cadence: 1 post/day minimum, 15 min/day replying. Photography X is real but niche — find the 50 wedding/portrait
  photographers and photo-business accounts with 5k–50k followers and become a familiar name in their replies.
- Formats that work here: mini-launch threads ("Shipped: OTP-gated galleries. Why galleries should need an
  email code in 2026 🧵"), money-math one-liners with a screenshot, quote-posts of public competitor pricing
  news (price hikes are public record — comment, don't attack), and polls ("What's your gallery stack? CRM +
  Pixieset / all-Sprout / HoneyBook now it has galleries / other").
- Every P0 roadmap item ships as a **mini-launch** (video + free-tier CTA). Six P0 items = six launch moments.
- Once: a **Show HN** ("Show HN: Snap – photographer studio platform with OTP-gated galleries, 0% commission")
  — hit-or-miss but free, and the security angle is HN-friendly. X ads: skip.

## 6. The solo-friendly operating system

**Daily (20 min):** FB groups answers + X replies + LinkedIn comments. No posting, just presence.

**Weekly:** Mon — 1 build note (LI + X, screenshot from the actual product). Wed — 1 money-math post
(graphic from the analysis tables). Fri — 1 demo clip (also posted to the FB page, later IG Reel).
One thread or LinkedIn article per week at most. One demo video per week.

**Monthly:** publish one artifact (comparison page live, calculator, exit guide); review UTM signups;
double down on the best pillar; retire what didn't move signups.

**Repurposing rule:** every asset is made once, posted three ways (X one-liner → LinkedIn short post →
FB group value post → IG Reel). Never write channel-native from scratch for all four.

## 7. First 30 days, concretely

- **Week 1 — assets:** demo gallery live; UTM convention in dashboard; switch-page stubs (start with Zenfolio,
  Táve/VSCO, Dubsado — the three loudest migration pools); stack-cost calculator sheet/page; profiles rewritten
  (clear one-liner + demo link).
- **Week 2 — presence:** join 10–15 FB groups, read rules, start answering (no links); follow/engage the 50 X
  accounts and 20 LinkedIn educators; first two build-in-public posts.
- **Week 3 — value:** first money-math posts (Dubsado hike, HoneyBook autopay rate, Pixieset free-tier 15%);
  first 60s demo clip (embed widget install); DM funnel live ("want me to import one gallery free?").
- **Week 4 — launch #1:** ship a P0 item publicly (annual billing or digital downloads) across all channels
  same-day; consider the first $5/day FB/IG test on the best clip; send the same launch to your website-client
  list (photographers among them get a personal note).

**90-day targets:** 100 signups with source attribution, ≥25% from social, ≥10 from FB-group threads,
1 switch page ranking, 3 demo videos published. If a channel produces zero signups in 90 days at honest
effort, cut it and reallocate to IG/SEO.

## 8. What NOT to do

- No corporate brand-voice posting; nobody follows a logo at 0 followers.
- No link-dropping in groups before you've answered questions — one ban poisons the channel.
- No named-competitor attack ads (organic honest comparisons only; keep the receipts factual — all the pricing
  claims in the analysis are cited and current).
- No spreading to 6 channels thin. FB groups + X + LinkedIn, with IG reposts, is the whole job until one channel
  proves out.
- Don't let the demo gallery rot — it's the front door; refresh the sample shoot quarterly.
